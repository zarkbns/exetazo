How it works

Four steps, about five seconds.

No chatbot to trust on faith. A scanner that shows its work and lets you check it.

01

Open a terms page

Any terms of service, privacy policy, or subscription agreement you're about to accept.

02

Click Scan

Exetazo reads the page and ignores the menus, banners, and cookie pop-ups.

03

Read the report

You get a score out of 100 and a list of risky clauses, worst first.

04

See the exact sentence

Tap a finding and the clause lights up on the page, with why it matters.

What it checks

15 risky clause types, in plain English.

Each one has a written definition and a fixed severity — nothing hidden. Tap a card to see what it means.

They can change the rules after you agree — sometimes without telling you. The deal you accepted can quietly become a different deal.

You give up your right to go to court. A private arbitrator decides instead, under rules the company chose.

You can't join a group lawsuit. If a company takes $5 from a million people, no one can realistically sue alone.

The subscription renews by itself. Miss the cancellation window and you're paying for another term.

Signing up took one click. Cancelling needs a phone call, a letter, or a maze of steps.

If the service breaks and you lose money, they cap what they owe you — sometimes at nothing.

You agree to pay their legal bills — even for disputes you didn't cause.

Your data goes to "partners", "affiliates", and "service providers". Those words can mean almost anyone.

The terms allow selling your personal information — or treat "sharing" and "selling" as the same thing.

It never says how long they keep your data — only "as long as necessary".

They can close your account for vague reasons — "harmful conduct", "any reason" — with no appeal.

Disputes must be settled in a court they picked — often far from where you live.

Prices can change mid-subscription, or extra charges show up later.

They claim wide rights over what you post — sometimes forever, even after you delete it.

One section promises something, another takes it back. Exetazo compares sections to catch the conflict.

Why Exetazo

Every flag shows its receipt.

Built like a security audit: fixed rules, quoted evidence, results you can reproduce.

You can check the evidence

Each finding quotes the sentence that triggered it and shows where it sits in the document. Nothing is flagged without a source.

The score is math, not an AI opinion

Start at 100 and subtract a fixed penalty per finding. Scan the same page twice and you get the same score — the AI never sets it.

100 − 20·critical − 10·high − 5·medium − 2·low

Nothing is kept

The page text is analyzed and thrown away. No account, no document archive, no tracking. The extension remembers only your last report, in session storage that clears when the browser closes.

What Exetazo is not.

It's a tool that points things out. It doesn't tell you what the law says about them.

Not legal advice. No attorney-client relationship, no opinion on whether a clause is legal or illegal.
Not a verdict. It flags potential risks worth a closer look. Jurisdiction, context, and exact wording all matter.
Not a chatbot. It doesn't answer questions about your contract. It audits the text and shows you what it found.

For anything important — a lease, a job contract, a settlement — talk to a lawyer. Exetazo is a second pair of eyes, not a substitute for one.
